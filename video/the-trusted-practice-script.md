# The Trusted Practice — Video Script

**Audience:** cosmetic dentists, dermatologists, aesthetic physicians, plastic surgeons and hair transplant surgeons (UAE, GCC, USA)
**Length:** about 13–14 minutes at a calm, natural pace
**Structure:** Opening (~2 min) · Part I The Doctor (~7.5 min) · Part II The Clinic (~1.5 min) · Part III The Systems (~2.5 min) · Close (~1 min)

If you’re a cosmetic dentist, a dermatologist, an aesthetic physician, a plastic surgeon or a hair transplant surgeon — in the UAE, the GCC or the US — this is for you. In the next thirteen minutes, I’ll walk you through three things: your **personal brand**, your **clinic brand**, and the **systems** that turn attention into booked consultations.

### Slide 2 — Why I’m making this · 0:35

I’m [Your name]. I’m a medical doctor, I coach high performers, and I build AI and automation systems. And I kept seeing the same thing: excellent clinicians — well trained, careful, ethical — losing patients to doctors who simply **communicate** better online. Not better medicine. Better communication, and better follow-up.

That gap is what I want to close. Not by turning you into an influencer — but by making the expertise you already have **visible**, and building the systems around it so no patient slips through.

### Slide 3 — The diagnosis · 1:10

So let’s start with a diagnosis. Most doctors think they have a content problem. / They don’t. They have a **pathway** problem. I see four leaks again and again.

**One:** attention without enquiries. Views and likes — but the messages never come, because nothing tells the patient what to do next.
**Two:** content without positioning. Your feed looks like every other clinic, so the patient has no specific reason to choose you.
**Three:** effort without a system. You post when there’s time, and stop when the clinic gets busy.
**Four:** consultations that start cold. The patient knows nothing about your approach, so you spend the first twenty minutes selling instead of consulting.

None of this is solved by posting more. / It’s solved by **structure**.

### Slide 4 — The three parts · 2:05

Here’s the structure. **Part one, the doctor:** your positioning, your content, how patients decide, and how they reach out. That’s the biggest part. **Part two, the clinic:** how a practice with several doctors builds one brand without anyone becoming invisible. And **part three, the systems:** what happens after a patient says “I’m interested” — because that’s where most clinics quietly lose them.

## PART I — THE DOCTOR

### Slide 5 — What a personal brand really is · 2:35

So, part one. What is a personal brand for a doctor? / It isn’t a logo. It isn’t your follower count. And it definitely isn’t dancing on Reels. It’s your **reputation — made visible before the first consultation.**

Think about it clinically. Your title tells a patient **what** you are. It doesn’t tell them how you think, what you’d recommend, what you’d refuse to do, or whether they’ll feel safe with you. That’s what an elective patient is really trying to find out — and that’s what your brand answers.

### Slide 6 — Positioning: the three Ps · 3:15

Everything starts with positioning — the work you do before you post a single thing. I use three questions. I call them the **three Ps.**

**Patient:** who exactly are you for? Age, concern, emotional state, what they’ve already tried. Not “anyone interested in aesthetics.”
**Procedure:** which one category do you want to own? Where your depth is real and your results are clearest.
**Philosophy:** what do you do differently, and why?

Hear the difference. Generic: *“Dr. Name offers a full range of hair restoration treatments in a welcoming environment.”* That tells a patient nothing. / Specific: *“I help men in their late twenties to forties plan their hair for the long term — not just the first surgery. I design hairlines for the face you’ll have at fifty.”* Now the right patient knows: **this is my doctor.**

Before you record another video — write that sentence.

### Slide 7 — Three convictions · 4:25

Then write **three convictions** — things you genuinely believe about your specialty that most colleagues don’t say out loud. They become the backbone of your content.

A cosmetic dentist: *“If your smile looks like veneers, the veneers have failed.”*
A dermatologist: *“Most skin I see is harmed by doing too much, not too little.”*
An aesthetic physician: *“Good work is invisible. People should notice you look rested — not that you’ve had something done.”*
A plastic surgeon: *“The most important part of surgery happens in the consultation.”*

One rule: they must be true to how you actually practise. / Patients can tell.

### Slide 8 — Five conversations · 5:15

Now, content. Instead of random posts, think of it as **five conversations**, each meeting a patient at a different stage.

**Name it** — describe the concern in the patient’s own words, so they think, “that’s me.”
**Explain it** — the *why* behind a treatment. This is your most valuable content, because it shows you’re a thinker, not a technician.
**Show it** — results and case stories, always with consent and context.
**Stand for it** — your philosophy: what you do, and what you won’t. It attracts the right patients and quietly filters out the wrong ones.
**Prove it** — reviews, credentials, your process, your team.

Here’s the insight. / Most doctors only post **“Show it”** — before-and-afters. That only speaks to patients who’ve already decided. The other four are where trust is built.

### Slide 9 — A rhythm you can sustain · 6:25

This doesn’t need to take over your life. The rhythm I recommend: **one ninety-minute filming session a week.** From that, you get four to five pieces. Short reels to name the concern. Longer videos and carousels to explain. Talking to camera for your philosophy. And stories for the invitation to book.

Where do the ideas come from? / Your consultation room. Every question a patient asks you this week is a piece of content. Have your team keep a running list — you’ll never run out.

### Slide 10 — How patients actually decide · 7:05

Now the part most doctors miss — **how patients actually decide.** Elective, appearance-related treatment is not an impulse buy. Patients often watch a doctor for **weeks, sometimes months,** before sending one message.

There are four stages. **Notice:** “This doctor keeps appearing.” **Research:** they go through your profile, highlights, reviews, location. **Decide:** “I want this — I just need a reason to act.” **Reach out:** the message or the booking — and by now, they arrive almost pre-sold.

That silent watching isn’t a problem. / It’s the opportunity. Your content works across that whole window — while you’re in clinic.

### Slide 11 — Turning interest into a conversation · 8:05

Which brings us to conversion. Use **three kinds of invitation.**
A **soft** one: *“Message me the word GUIDE and I’ll send you the three questions I ask before recommending this.”*
A **qualifying** one: *“If you’ve had this done elsewhere and you’re not happy, message me — it’s often correctable.”*
And a **direct** one, used sparingly: *“If you’ve been thinking about this for months, that’s your sign. The link to book is in my bio.”*

When the message comes in, keep it to **three steps**: acknowledge and ask one question; give one genuinely helpful, non-diagnostic insight; then offer the consultation — no pressure.

And measure what matters. Not followers. Not likes. **Enquiries, consultations booked, show-up rate, treatment acceptance.** A few hundred views with real enquiries will build a practice. / Fifty thousand views with none won’t.

### Slide 12 — Who leads: you or the clinic? · 9:15

One decision before we move on: who leads — **you, or the clinic?** If you own the practice, lead with your personal brand. Patients trust people before they trust buildings, and the clinic grows from your standards. If you’re one of three, four or five doctors, the **clinic** leads — and every doctor is clearly featured inside it.

## PART II — THE CLINIC

### Slide 13 — What a clinic brand really is · 9:40

Part two: the clinic brand. It isn’t your logo or your interior. / It’s the **experience** a patient has — from the first message at eleven at night, to the follow-up three months later.

In a multi-doctor clinic, give each doctor a clear **role** on the clinic’s channels. The **explainer**, who answers the specialty’s common questions. The **guide**, who shows what happens before, during and after treatment. The **voice**, who shares a clinical philosophy or special interest. Everyone keeps their own name, credentials and personality. Patients see a team of distinct experts — and the clinic never depends on one person.

### Slide 14 — The clinic operating rhythm · 10:30

To keep it running, you need a simple rhythm. **Define** the clinic promise — who you serve, your tone, and what you’ll never claim. **Map** each doctor’s focus. **Plan** a shared calendar that rotates the spotlight fairly. And **assign owners** — who films, who checks accuracy and consent, who posts, and, critically, **who answers the messages.**

Because a brand is only as good as the experience behind it. / If a post says “message us,” someone has to reply — fast. And that brings us to part three.

---

## PART III — THE SYSTEMS

### Slide 15 — Where clinics lose patients · 11:10

Here’s an uncomfortable truth. / Most clinics don’t have a lead problem. They have a **follow-up** problem.

Messages scattered across Instagram, WhatsApp, the website and the phone. Replies that take hours — while the patient has already messaged two other clinics. Consultations that end with “I’ll think about it,” and no follow-up. No-shows. And past patients who would happily come back — but nobody invites them.

Every one of those is a patient you’ve already **paid** to attract.

### Slide 16 — The patient pathway: capture and convert · 11:45

So this is what I build with doctors: a **patient pathway** — seven connected systems.
**One — one inbox.** Every enquiry from every channel lands in one place, with an owner.
**Two — instant response.** An AI assistant replies within a minute, day or night: answers practical questions, asks qualifying questions, and hands over to your team. It never gives medical advice.
**Three — booking and reminders,** so patients book easily, and actually turn up.
**Four — consultation follow-up.** A respectful sequence for everyone who said, “let me think about it.”

### Slide 17 — The patient pathway: keep and grow · 12:25

**Five — reviews.** Every patient is invited, fairly, to share their experience — and any concern goes privately to the right person.
**Six — reactivation.** Consent-based WhatsApp updates, for example once a month, and timely reminders when a treatment is due for review.
**Seven — a dashboard.** One screen that shows where patients come from, how fast they’re answered, and how many book, attend and go ahead.

The point isn’t more technology. / It’s that **no patient who raised a hand is ever forgotten** — and your team spends less time chasing.

### Slide 18 — Guardrails · 13:00

And because we’re doctors, the guardrails matter. Automation handles **admin — never clinical judgement.** Anything clinical or urgent goes straight to a human. Messages only go to people who’ve agreed to receive them, with a clear way to opt out. Data is kept to a minimum. And everything follows your local rules — your health authority in the UAE or GCC, or HIPAA and your state board in the US. We start with **one bottleneck**, prove it works, then expand.
